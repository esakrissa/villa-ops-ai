# VillaOps Privacy Policy

Last updated: 1 November 2026. This policy explains how VillaOps Bali ("we", "us") collects, uses and protects personal data of guests who book stays at the villas we manage, and of property owners who use our platform. It is written to comply with Indonesia's Personal Data Protection Law (Law No. 27 of 2022, "UU PDP").

## Who we are

VillaOps Bali manages private villas and guesthouses in Ubud, Canggu, Seminyak and Uluwatu on behalf of their owners. For guest bookings we act as the data controller. Our data protection officer can be reached at privacy@villaops.example.

## Data we collect

### Booking data

When a guest books, we collect their full name, email address, phone or WhatsApp number, nationality, number of guests, arrival and departure dates, and any special requests (for example dietary needs, airport pickup or a baby cot).

### Identity documents

Indonesian immigration rules require accommodation providers to report foreign guests. At check-in we take a copy of each adult guest's passport photo page. We do not collect any other identity document and we never ask guests to send passport copies over social media.

### Payment data

Card payments are processed by Stripe. We never see or store full card numbers; we only keep the last four digits, the card brand and the payment status. Bank transfer guests share their transfer receipt, which we store with the booking.

### Data collected automatically

Our booking website uses essential cookies for login and checkout, and privacy-friendly analytics without cross-site tracking. We do not use advertising cookies.

## How we use personal data

We use guest data only to: confirm and manage bookings; arrange check-in, transport and housekeeping; meet the immigration reporting obligation; send pre-arrival information and the post-stay review request; and handle complaints or damage claims. We do not sell personal data and we do not use it for third-party advertising.

Marketing emails are sent only to guests who opted in at booking. Every email has a one-click unsubscribe link.

## Sharing with third parties

We share the minimum data needed with: the villa owner (guest name, dates and number of guests only); our drivers and tour partners (name, pickup time and WhatsApp number); Stripe for payments; and the Indonesian immigration office (foreign guest report). All partners sign a data processing agreement.

## Data retention

- Booking records are kept for 5 years after check-out to meet Indonesian tax and accounting rules.
- Passport copies are deleted 30 days after check-out, unless there is an open damage claim or a legal request, in which case they are kept until it is resolved.
- WhatsApp conversations with guests are archived after 90 days and deleted after 1 year.
- Marketing consent records are kept as long as the guest stays subscribed, plus 1 year.
- CCTV footage from villa entrances (no cameras inside villas or bathrooms, ever) is overwritten automatically after 14 days.

## Guest rights

Under UU PDP, guests can ask to access, correct or delete their personal data, withdraw marketing consent, and object to processing. Send requests to privacy@villaops.example. We reply within 3 working days and complete the request within 14 days. Some data (booking records needed for tax) cannot be deleted before the retention period ends; we will explain this in our reply.

## Security

Guest data is stored in encrypted databases hosted in Singapore. Staff access is role-based and logged; villa staff only see bookings for their own villa. Passport copies are stored in a separate encrypted bucket that only the front office manager can open.

## Data breaches

If a breach affects guest data, we notify affected guests and the authorities within 72 hours (3 x 24 hours) of becoming aware of it, as UU PDP requires, describing what happened, what data was involved and what we are doing about it.

## Changes to this policy

We review this policy every year. Material changes are emailed to guests with an upcoming booking at least 14 days before they take effect.
